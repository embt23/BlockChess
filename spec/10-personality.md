# 10 — The personality layer

*The thesis of the project. Everything else is machinery for it.*

---

## The claim, in the author's words

> The maths behind blockchain/hashing stems from ways of encoding/layering
> information. What separates my method for "encrypting" the information that
> exists within a chess game does not come from the geometry of the game, but
> the geometry of how different people play.
>
> Every match is compressed based on previous matches.
> **Repetition → Compression → personality.**
>
> Objects don't exist on their own, they exist in relationship to other objects.
> The position of the game in space exists as the representation of the
> relationship between the players. Skill isn't an area, it is the ability to
> use tactical balance / be in the right place at the right time.

This file is the technical account of why that is correct, where it needs
amending, and what it means the system has to be able to do.

---

## 1. Why the game carries no information, and the players carry all of it

Both players know the rules. The legal move function is common knowledge. In the
Shannon sense **common knowledge has zero surprisal** — nothing is learned by
being told something you already know.

So the entire information content of a chess game lies in *which* move was
chosen from the legal set. And that choice is generated by a policy:

```
    π : Position → Distribution over legal moves
```

A game is therefore a **sample from the joint behaviour of two policies**. The
board is the channel; the players are the source.

That is the precise form of "not the geometry of the game, but the geometry of
how different people play", and it is exactly right. The game tree is public,
fixed, and shared. The policy is private, learned, and yours.

**The policy is the asset. The game is only evidence of it.**

---

## 2. Correction: style exists because of bounded rationality, not because chess is unsolvable

> *"You can't find an exploit, because every move has a counter."*

This needs amending, and the amendment strengthens the thesis rather than
weakening it.

**Zermelo's theorem (1913)** says chess is *determined*: exactly one of — White
can force a win, Black can force a win, or both can force a draw — is true. A
perfect strategy exists. There is an exploit; every move does *not* have a
counter in the game-theoretic sense.

What is true is that the solution is **computationally unreachable**. About
10⁴⁴ legal positions, a game tree near 10¹²⁰. No physical process will ever
enumerate it.

Why the correction matters: it relocates the source of value. Style does not
exist because the game is inexhaustible. Style exists in the **gap between the
optimal policy and what a bounded agent can compute.** Every real player —
human or engine — plays an approximation, and the *shape of their approximation*
is their personality.

The evidence is checkers. Schaeffer solved it in 2007. It is a draw. There is no
checkers style economy, and there never will be again, because the gap closed.

> **The thesis depends on chess remaining unsolved, not on chess being
> unsolvable.** State it that way and it is safe for the lifetime of the
> universe; state it the other way and it is false.

---

## 3. What the asymmetries actually contribute

The listed imbalances are real: White moves first; the king and queen sit on
different files so the two wings are not mirror images; kingside and queenside
castling are genuinely different operations; pawns move one way, capture
another, move two squares once, take en passant, and promote to any piece — so
that half the pieces on the board each carry the latent geometry of all the
others.

But asymmetry is not what creates the gap in §2. Complexity creates the gap.

What asymmetry does is give the gap **shape**.

In a symmetric, low-dimensional game, good strategies cluster toward a single
equilibrium and "skill" collapses to one number. Chess's asymmetries carve the
strategy space into distinct regions — open versus closed positions, structures,
initiative versus material, endgame classes — each rewarding a different
competence. So the space of *good approximations* is high-dimensional and
structured rather than a single point.

That is what makes personality multidimensional instead of scalar:

> Complexity makes style **possible**. Asymmetry makes style **plural**.

---

## 4. Personality, measured in bits

This is the part that turns the intuition into an equation. It is exact, not
analogy.

Encoding data drawn from distribution `P` using a code built for distribution
`Q` costs

```
    H(P) + D(P ‖ Q)      bits
```

Encoding it with a code built for `P` itself costs `H(P)`. So the extra bits you
pay for using the wrong model are exactly the Kullback–Leibler divergence.

Now apply it. Take a player's games. Compress them with `π_pop`, a model of how
the population plays. Then compress them with `π_you`, a model of how *you*
play. The saving is:

```
    bits saved per move  =  D( π_you ‖ π_pop )
```

> ### Your personality, in bits per move, is exactly what you save by being compressed as yourself.

"Repetition → Compression → personality" is not a metaphor. Repetition is what
lets a model of you be fitted at all; compression is the operation that measures
it; the measured quantity is a divergence.

**Personality is not a thing. It is a difference.**

---

## 5. The four divergences

Everything of value in this project turns out to be the same operation applied
to different arguments.

| Quantity | Is | Where |
|---|---|---|
| **Edge** — your bankroll growth rate | `D(p ‖ r)` — your belief about the outcome against the odds-implied belief | Kelly; `06-economics.md` §4 |
| **Identity** — how much you are *you* | `D(π_you ‖ π_pop)` — your policy against the population's | §4 above |
| **Evidence** — proof someone is cheating | `D(π_observed ‖ π_claimed)` — drift rate of the sequential test | `06-economics.md` §5 |
| **Value** — what a style model is worth to a buyer | `D(posterior ‖ prior)` — how far it moves their beliefs | §7 below |

> ### Everything worth money here is a divergence between two models of the same thing.

That is the thesis sentence of the project and of the series. Four chapters of
apparently unrelated material — betting theory, compression, hypothesis testing,
information economics — are one function with different inputs.

It also collapses two layers that looked separate:

> **The cheat detector and the style asset are the same mathematical object.**
> A cheat detector is a model of you that says *"this does not look like you."*
> A style asset is a model of you that says *"this is what I look like."*

Build one and you have built the other. `06-economics.md` §5 and this file are
describing one piece of software.

## 6. Elo is the transitive part. Style is the rest.

> *"Skill isn't an area, it is the ability to use tactical balance / be in the
> right place at the right time."*

This is correct and it has a sharp technical form.

Elo is a **rank-one model**. It assigns each player a single number and predicts
every matchup from the difference. By construction it cannot represent a cycle:
if A beats B and B beats C, Elo must predict A beats C.

Real competitive games are not rank-one. Their matchup matrices have a
transitive component *and* a cyclic one — rock-paper-scissors structure among
players of equal strength. (Balduzzi et al., *Re-evaluating Evaluation*, 2018,
call the resulting shape a "spinning top": a broad cyclic base narrowing toward
a transitive peak.)

```
    matchup matrix   =   transitive component   +   cyclic component
                              ↑                          ↑
                             Elo                       STYLE
```

> **Elo is the projection of skill onto one axis. Personality is the residual —
> and the residual is precisely the part Elo was built to discard.**

Two consequences the project should act on:

1. **A single rating is lossy on purpose.** `07-servers.md` already prefers
   Glicko-2 for its uncertainty term; the deeper point is that *no* scalar can
   express a matchup cycle. A style vector can.
2. **Non-transitivity is where the money is.** If ratings determined outcomes,
   there would be nothing to know. Cycles mean "who beats whom" is not implied
   by the leaderboard — and information that is not implied by public data is
   the definition of an exploitable edge.

This also rescues `06-economics.md` §4 from its own conclusion. That section
showed near-even wagering is an almost-zero-capacity channel: +7 Elo needs ~3,500
games to double a bankroll. True — *along the transitive axis*. The cyclic
component is exactly the part a rating cannot price, so it is exactly where a
real edge can survive.

## 7. The style channel dwarfs the outcome channel

An order-of-magnitude argument. The inputs are estimates and labelled as such;
the conclusion is robust to being wrong about them by a lot.

**What a game reveals about who won.** Win/draw/loss is at most `log₂ 3 ≈ 1.6`
bits, realistically ~1.4 given draw rates. Of that, the *economically
exploitable* part at near-even odds is the Kelly growth from §5 — at +7 Elo,
**0.0003 bits per game**.

**What a game reveals about who you are.** `06-economics.md` §5 uses
`D_KL ≈ 0.10` nats/move for a full engine substitution. A human's divergence from
the population mean is smaller — call it `0.02–0.10` nats/move. Over ~80 plies:

```
    0.05 nats/move × 80 plies  ≈  4 nats  ≈  5.8 bits of identity per game
```

| Channel | Bits per game |
|---|---|
| Who you are | ~6 |
| Who won | ~1.4 |
| Who won, *exploitable* at +7 Elo | ~0.0003 |

These are not fungible bits and the comparison is not a price. But the
structural fact survives any plausible revision of the estimates:

> **A chess game emits roughly four orders of magnitude more information about
> the players than about the result.**

The project has so far been built entirely around the 0.0003. The 6 is sitting
there untouched. That is the argument for the personality layer, and it is the
author's intuition made quantitative.

## 8. The three layers, as the author frames them

> *From Divine Chaos (creation & destruction) came order (safety & tyranny).
> Then from the marriage of chaos and order came the world of gods
> (Symbol/experience).* — after Peterson, *Maps of Meaning*

Recorded as the author's framing, in his words, because he holds it and it is
the shape he thinks in. What follows is a translation into the project's terms,
offered alongside rather than as a correction. **The architecture does not
depend on the metaphysics** — which is a feature, since it means the engineering
stands for people who do not share the frame.

| Frame | In this project | Where it lives |
|---|---|---|
| **Chaos** — undifferentiated possibility | The legal move space. 10⁴⁴ positions. Uncomputable, unstructured, everything that *could* happen. | `bc-chess` — the rules define the space |
| **Order** — constraint, law, enforcement | The protocol. What is permitted, what is signed, what is punished. | the chain, channels, the adjudicator |
| **Symbol** — the compressed representation an agent actually carries | The policy `π`. Not the tree and not the rulebook, but the learned abstraction a bounded agent navigates by. | **does not exist yet** |

The third row is the whole point. A policy *is* a compression of experience into
a decision function — which is what a model is, in the precise
information-theoretic sense. The layer the project is missing is the layer where
the compressed representation becomes an object you can hold, prove, price, and
trade.

## 9. Prior art — this is established, not speculative

The style layer rests on published results, which matters for a project whose
standing rule is that every layer is checked against work someone else did.

- **Maia** (McIlroy-Young et al., NeurIPS 2020) — networks trained to predict
  *human* moves at a target rating rather than best moves, reaching ~50%
  move-match. Human style is learnable.
- **Individual behaviour** (McIlroy-Young et al., KDD 2022) — identifying a
  specific player from their moves alone. Style is not just learnable, it is a
  *fingerprint*.
- **Regan & Haworth**, intrinsic chess ratings — the policy-model basis of
  serious cheat detection, and already cited in `06-economics.md` §5.
- **Balduzzi et al.**, *Re-evaluating Evaluation* (2018) — the transitive/cyclic
  decomposition behind §6.

## 10. Risks, stated plainly

1. **Demand is unproven.** Chess preparation is a real activity people pay for,
   and a model of a specific opponent is what preparation *is*. But no market
   for it exists today, and building one is a bet.
2. **Style depreciates.** Once your model is public, opponents prepare against
   it and your edge decays. That is realistic — it is what happens to real
   grandmasters — but it means style is a licence with a shelf life, not land.
3. **Information is copyable.** Sell a model and the buyer can resell it. This
   is the fundamental defect of information markets and it needs a mechanism,
   not a hope. See `11-resources.md` §4.
4. **The estimate in §7 is still an estimate.** The *instrument* now exists and
   is calibrated — see `docs/d20-calibration.md` — but it has not yet been
   pointed at humans. The estimator recovers ~60% of a known divergence, so any
   figure it returns is a **lower bound**; it reads +0.0006 nats/move on a
   dataset with no player behind it, so it does not fabricate signal; and it
   needs **hundreds of games per player** before moderate style is visible.
   Until it runs on a real corpus, §7 remains a prediction.

## 11. What this changes

- The economics chapter's conclusion — that near-even wagering is near-zero
  capacity — stands, and now has a resolution: the value is in the information
  layer, not the outcome layer.
- The cheat detector and the style model are one deliverable, not two.
- Ratings need to become **vectors**, not scalars, at the server layer.
- The project acquires a second act. Act I makes the *game* trustless. Act II
  makes the *player* an object. See `docs/atlas.md`.
